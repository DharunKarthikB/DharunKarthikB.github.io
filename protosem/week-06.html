Geometric Horse Design

Laser Cutting and Engraving

Project Overview

This project involved the modification and fabrication of a geometric horse artwork using RDWorks V8. The design features an intricate arrangement of triangular patterns that collectively form the silhouette of a horse. An existing design was selected and modified to achieve the desired appearance and prepare it for laser processing.

The modified artwork was processed on black acrylic measuring 50 × 50 mm using both laser cutting and engraving techniques. The project demonstrates the application of digital design preparation and laser processing in transforming a computer-generated pattern into a physical decorative artwork.

Objective

The primary objective of this project was to modify an existing geometric design and convert it into a physical artwork using laser cutting and engraving. The project also aimed to develop practical familiarity with RDWorks V8, understand the preparation of designs for laser processing, and observe the accuracy and detailing achievable through laser fabrication.

  • Software Used: RDWorks V8
  • Material: Black Acrylic
  • Dimensions: 50 × 50 mm
  • Processing Techniques: Laser Cutting and Engraving

Design and Modification

The project began with an existing geometric horse design consisting of multiple interconnected triangular shapes. The original artwork was imported into RDWorks V8, where modifications were made to suit the intended output.

The design was reviewed and adjusted while maintaining its overall horse silhouette and geometric pattern. Particular attention was given to the arrangement of the individual shapes and the overall appearance of the artwork to ensure that the modified design retained its visual structure.

Design Preparation and Execution

After completing the modifications, the design was prepared in RDWorks V8 for laser processing. The software's preview interface was used to inspect the arrangement of the artwork and review the intended processing layout before execution.

The prepared design was then processed on a 50 × 50 mm black acrylic sheet using laser cutting and engraving. The cutting process helped define the required shapes and outlines, while engraving contributed to the detailed appearance of the geometric artwork.

Final Output

The final output was a geometric horse artwork featuring an intricate triangular pattern on black acrylic. The completed piece successfully translated the modified digital design into a physical output while retaining the distinctive silhouette and geometric detailing of the original artwork.

This project provided practical exposure to digital design modification, laser processing preparation, and the use of RDWorks V8 for producing detailed acrylic-based artwork.

Skills and Learning Outcomes

  • Gained practical familiarity with RDWorks V8 for preparing designs for laser processing.
  • Developed experience in modifying existing digital artwork to achieve a desired output.
  • Understood the basic workflow involved in preparing and previewing designs before laser execution.
  • Explored the application of laser cutting and engraving on acrylic materials.
  • Gained exposure to the conversion of digital geometric patterns into physical decorative products.

3D Printing – Pocket Knife Component Fabrication

Project Overview

This project involved the modification and 3D printing of a pocket knife model using Bambu Studio. The design consists of multiple individual components that are intended to be assembled into a complete pocket knife structure.

An existing 3D model was selected and modified to suit the intended output. The modified components were prepared for additive manufacturing using PLA filament with a 15% infill density. The project demonstrates the application of 3D printing in producing detailed components for a compact mechanical assembly.

Objective

The primary objective of this project was to modify an existing pocket knife model and manufacture its individual components using 3D printing. The project also aimed to develop practical familiarity with Bambu Studio, understand slicing parameters, and explore the process of preparing multiple components for subsequent assembly.

  • Software Used: Bambu Studio
  • Material: PLA (Polylactic Acid)
  • Infill Density: 15%
  • Material Consumption: 20.75 grams (Estimated)
  • Printing Time: 1 hour 24 minutes (Estimated)
  • Manufacturing Process: Fused Deposition Modeling (FDM)
  • Final Assembly: Multiple printed components assembled into a pocket knife structure

Design and Modification

The project began with an existing 3D model of a pocket knife consisting of several individual components. The original model was imported and modified to suit the intended fabrication requirements.

The components were reviewed and arranged to maintain their intended geometry and assembly relationships. The design was prepared with consideration for the individual parts required to construct the complete model.

Slicing and Print Preparation

Bambu Studio was used to prepare the modified pocket knife components for 3D printing. The digital model was processed through the slicing workflow, converting its geometry into instructions for the 3D printer.

A 15% infill density was selected for the printing process, and PLA filament was used as the manufacturing material. The slicing preview was reviewed to inspect the arrangement of the components and evaluate the estimated material consumption and printing duration.

The software estimated a total material requirement of approximately 20.75 grams and a printing duration of 1 hour and 24 minutes.

3D Printing Process and Assembly

The prepared components were manufactured using the Fused Deposition Modeling (FDM) process, in which thermoplastic filament is deposited layer by layer to form the required geometry. PLA filament was used to produce the individual pocket knife components according to the slicing instructions generated by Bambu Studio.

The printed components were then assembled into the complete pocket knife structure. The individual parts were designed to come together to form the overall model, demonstrating how multiple 3D-printed components can be fabricated separately and combined into a single assembly.

Project Outcome

The project involved the successful preparation and 3D printing of individual pocket knife components using a modified digital model. The printed parts were assembled into the complete structure.

Through this project, practical experience was gained in modifying existing 3D models, preparing components for additive manufacturing, configuring slicing parameters, and understanding the relationship between digital design, material consumption, printing time, and component assembly.

Skills and Learning Outcomes

  • Gained practical familiarity with Bambu Studio for 3D model preparation and slicing.
  • Developed experience in modifying existing 3D models for additive manufacturing.
  • Understood the importance of component arrangement and preparation for subsequent assembly.
  • Explored the application of PLA filament in producing detailed 3D-printed components.
  • Gained exposure to the FDM manufacturing process and layer-by-layer fabrication.
  • Learned to interpret material consumption and printing time estimates from slicing software.
  • Developed an understanding of how separately manufactured components can be combined into a complete mechanical assembly.
